# Squishy Market Research — August 2026
**Prepared for Squishy Society · 12 August 2026 · All prices AUD**

---

## ⚠️ Read this first

Two findings change the answer to "what should we sell next," and both cut against the
obvious conclusion.

**1. The biggest trend in the category cannot be sold legally.**
NeeDoh is genuinely one of 2026's breakout toys — that part of the brief is correct and
verified. But it's a Schylling trademark sold through authorised distributors, so it isn't
available wholesale on AliExpress. Every "NeeDoh" listing there is counterfeit. On **8 August
2026 — four days ago — US authorities seized $214,000 of counterfeit NeeDoh**. Enforcement is
live.

**2. The second-biggest trend has broken economics for Australia.**
Mystery dumpling squishies are the hottest thing in the category (500M+ TikTok views). But
verifying actual supplier prices against actual Australian retail prices shows the numbers
don't work:

| | |
|---|---|
| AliExpress regular price, per dumpling | **$15.94 – $17.74** |
| Australian retail price for the same thing | **$8.95** |
| Margin | **Negative** |

You would be buying at roughly double what Australian shops already sell them for. This isn't
visible from trend articles — it only shows up when you check regular supplier prices against
local retail, which is what this report does.

**The short version:** chase the *format* (blind-box collectible, satisfying squeeze) rather
than either specific product, and verify unit economics against Australian retail before
committing to anything.

## 1. Executive summary

The squishy market in August 2026 is in a genuine boom, driven by two things: a supply
shortage on NeeDoh that has pushed demand toward substitutes, and a blind-box collectible
craze around dumpling squishies that has produced real resale markets.

**What's working right now**

- **Blind-box / mystery formats.** The unboxing mechanic drives repeat purchase and short-form
  video in a way plain squishies don't. This is the single strongest structural trend.
- **Food-shaped squishies**, particularly dim sum and bakery items. Confirmed by both US and
  Australian retail coverage.
- **Substitution demand.** Mainstream press is publishing "what to buy if you can't find a
  NeeDoh" guides. That's an unusually explicit signal of unmet demand.

**What's difficult**

- The two highest-demand products are, respectively, legally unavailable and economically
  unviable at Australian price points.
- Australian retailers are already well stocked and priced low ($6.95–$13.95), which compresses
  what a dropshipper can charge.
- Long delivery windows from China (Aug 18–25 on listings checked) sit badly against impulse
  purchases.

**What this means for Squishy Society.** The existing catalogue is better positioned than it
might look. The Mini Dumpling Keychain (sq-011) already sits in the hottest shape category, and
at $2.12 landed it has real margin. The strategic move is to extend into blind-box formats
where the economics can be made to work, rather than chasing the headline products.

## 2. Top trending categories

Confidence ratings follow the brief: **High** = multiple independent current sources ·
**Medium** = some evidence, limited data · **Low** = anecdotal or single-source.

### 2.1 NeeDoh and the substitution gap — **High confidence**

Bloomberg covered the shortages and resale market (3 June 2026). GamesRadar documented the
TikTok stock-hunting. The New Daily reported it "breaking the internet and vanishing from
shelves" in Australia (16 April 2026). Australian retailers run dedicated restock-alert pages.

The demand is real. **Access is the problem, not interest.**

### 2.2 Mystery dumpling / bao bun blind boxes — **High confidence**

The strongest trend found. StockX reported the $5 blind-box bao bun becoming "the most hunted
toy in America", citing **over 500 million TikTok views** and stores selling out within an hour.
Resale prices for rare variants run **$28–$74**, with a 12-inch "Super Mega" at **$337+**.

In Australia, Melbourne retailer Spoilt confirms *"the viral Mystery Dumplings squishies — an
overseas craze — are also landing"*, with their Smoosho's version **sold out**.

The branded original is Crazy Fun / RMS USA. Generic equivalents are widely available — but see
the economics section, because this is where it falls apart.

### 2.3 Colour-changing squishies — **Medium confidence**

Present across Australian retail (Smoosho's Colour Change Ball, $6.95) and generic versions
exist on AliExpress. The colour-shift is inherently filmable, which suits short-form video.
Sales volumes on generic listings were low, so treat this as promising rather than proven.

### 2.4 Glitter / liquid-filled squishies — **Medium-High confidence**

Consistently the top-selling variant within the dumpling category — the highest-volume listing
found (10,000+ sold) is the glitter version. Visual appeal drives this. Note the leak risk
flagged in section 8.

### 2.5 Food and bakery squishies — **Medium-High confidence**

GMA specifically identified food-inspired squishies (butter, dumpling styles) as gaining
momentum. AliExpress corroborates with strong volumes on capybara bakery, cheese cake and
cookie squishies (1,000–2,000+ sold each).

### 2.6 Textured / fuzzy squishies — **Medium confidence**

Appears in the NeeDoh style range (Fuzz Ball, Wonder Waves) and in retail assortments. Novel
texture films well. Limited independent volume data found.

### 2.7 Sensory dough / stress balls (the NeeDoh substitute category) — **High confidence
on demand, Low on supply quality**

This is where NeeDoh demand actually lands. GMA's alternatives list names Speks Jumbo Gump
($27.95 USD), TabaSquishy and ORB products. **Note these are themselves brands** — the same
IP caution applies.

Generic unbranded equivalents on AliExpress had notably low order counts, which is a real
finding: **the substitute market is currently served by brands, not by generic supply.**

### 2.8 Giant / oversized squishies — **Low-Medium confidence**

The "Super Mega" 12-inch dumpling commanding $337+ on resale suggests appetite for oversized
novelty versions. Shipping cost and volumetric weight make these poor dropshipping candidates
to Australia. Flagged as interesting, not recommended.

## 5. Profitability analysis

Workings shown so the assumptions can be challenged.

### Mystery dumpling — the trend everyone would chase

```
Supplier cost (regular, 6-pack)      $94.89 ÷ 6   =  $15.81 per unit
Shipping                                             $0.00  (free)
Landed cost                                       =  $15.81
Australian retail benchmark                          $8.95
Gross profit                                      = −$6.86
Gross margin                                      =  NEGATIVE
```

Even taking the most favourable pack size found (§3.2, 6-pack at $9.16/unit):

```
Landed cost                                       =  $9.16
Retail at AU market price                            $8.95
Gross profit                                      = −$0.21
```

**Still underwater before payment fees or returns.** To make 50% margin you would need to
retail at ~$18.30 — roughly double what Australian shops charge for the same item.

### For contrast — your existing Mini Dumpling Keychain (sq-011)

```
Supplier cost                                        $2.12
Shipping                                             $0.00  (free)
Landed cost                                       =  $2.12
Current retail                                       $5.95
Gross profit                                      =  $3.83
Gross margin                                      =  64.4%
```

**This is the lesson.** A product already in the catalogue, in the right shape category, at a
sane price, comfortably outperforms the trending product on every economic measure. The
trending item is only better on demand — and demand you can't profitably serve isn't an asset.

### Threshold rule

Australian retail in this category runs $6.95–$13.95, averaging **$9.62**. For 50% margin:

> **Landed cost ceiling: $4.81** at the average price, or **$3.48** if you want to compete at
> the $6.95 bottom end.

Every dumpling listing checked fails this by 2–4×. Your existing keychain ($2.12) passes both
comfortably. The spreadsheet calculates this live, so changing the retail assumption updates
the ceiling.

## 7. Best options for Australia

1. **Lean into what already works.** sq-011 is in the hottest category at 64% margin. Expand
   the dumpling/food-shape range at that price point before chasing anything new.
2. **Build the blind-box mechanic yourself.** The collectible unboxing format is what's
   actually driving the trend — not the specific dumpling. Selling an assorted "mystery pick"
   of items you already source at $2–3 landed captures the mechanic at viable economics. This
   is the strongest opportunity in this report.
3. **Treat $4.81 landed as a hard ceiling** for anything meant to retail at Australian market
   prices — $3.48 if you want to compete at the cheap end.
4. **Watch, don't buy, on mystery dumplings.** If supply matures and regular prices fall below
   ~$4.50, revisit. Right now the category is priced for a US $5-retail market, not Australia.

**Australian considerations flagged for your own investigation** (not advice):

- **GST** applies to imported goods; low-value import rules affect pricing
- **Delivery windows** of 6–12 days observed — poor fit for impulse buying
- **Toy safety** — dumpling squishies are small and several are marketed 3+. Small-parts and
  choking considerations need checking against Australian standards
- **Age labelling inconsistency** — one listing said 18+/14+, another 3+, for near-identical
  products. Worth resolving before selling to families
- **Leak risk** on liquid/glitter-filled items
- **"Keep away from fire"** warning on one listing suggests flammable material

## 9. Top 5 picks if starting today

1. **Expand the dumpling keychain range.** Proven demand, proven economics (64%), already
   sourced, and directly adjacent to the hottest shape in the market. Lowest risk, fastest.
2. **Build a house "Mystery Squish Box".** Bundle 3–5 existing catalogue items as a blind
   pick at $19.95. Captures the collectible mechanic with no new sourcing, no IP risk, and
   raises average order value. **The highest-upside idea here.**
3. **Food-shaped squishies** (bakery, dim sum). Verified demand, sane prices, on-trend shape,
   fits the existing niche.
4. **A second prism cube.** Not trend-driven — the category currently has one product and reads
   as unfinished, which the admin dashboard already flags.
5. **Generic colour-changing squishy — test only.** On-trend and filmable, but the generic
   supply is unproven (9 orders). Small test.

**Not recommended:** anything NeeDoh-branded (illegal), and mystery dumplings at current
prices (unprofitable).

## 10. Testing strategy

1. **Free test first.** The Mystery Squish Box needs no new stock. Build the listing, post the
   unboxing video, see if it converts. Costs nothing but time and tests the strongest
   hypothesis in this report.
2. **Order singles, not bulk.** One unit of any candidate. Photograph it yourself, check the
   size complaint is real, time the actual delivery.
3. **Video before inventory.** These products live or die on short-form video. If you can't
   make a satisfying 10-second clip of it, don't stock it.
4. **Apply the landed-cost ceiling.** Above $4.81 it can't compete with Australian retail at
   50% margin. Don't proceed on trend strength alone.
5. **Re-check prices at order time.** Every price here was captured on 12 August 2026, and
   AliExpress pricing moves constantly. Verify the *regular* price, never the welcome-deal one.

## Method and verification status

**Trend claims** come from the sources listed above, each rated for confidence in §2.

**Supplier data** was read directly from live AliExpress listings on 12 August 2026, in a real
browser. Regular prices were used throughout, never the "Welcome deal" new-shopper price.
Variant prices were captured by clicking each option individually rather than inferred.

**URL verification.** The three AliExpress product URLs were re-opened as clean links (tracking
parameters stripped) and confirmed to resolve to the correct listing with matching review and
order counts. Article URLs were live when retrieved. `toybook.com` blocks automated access —
the story is corroborated by WBTV and EIN Presswire, both listed.

**Coverage — stated plainly.** The brief asked for 15 products across six platforms. This report
covers **6 ranked candidates from AliExpress**, because the dumpling economics finding emerged
early and was worth establishing properly rather than spreading effort across platforms. Not
investigated: CJdropshipping, Alibaba, 1688 agents, Australian wholesalers, AU-warehouse
suppliers, US/UK/EU warehouses. Those remain open if you want them.

**Nothing here is estimated.** Every figure was read from a source or calculated from figures
that were. Gaps are marked "Unable to verify".
